## How Warhammer Game Release Dates Get Announced

Licensed games rarely appear out of nowhere. They move through recognizable stages, and each stage gives you a different level of confidence. Knowing the sequence helps you judge how close a launch really is.

| Stage | What typically happens | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Tease | Logo or cinematic, no date attached | Official social channels |
| Store page | Product listing goes live as "coming soon" | PC and console storefronts |
| Gameplay reveal | Trailer with real footage | Publisher showcase or livestream |
| Window announcement | A season or year is named | Press release, official site |
| Firm date | Exact day plus preorder details | Official channels, store pages |
| Launch | Unlock times by region | Store page and launch FAQ |

The most volatile stretch is the gap between a named window and a firm date. That is when delays are announced, when placeholder listings get edited, and when fake dates spread fastest. If you see a specific day floating around without a trailer behind it, assume it is a placeholder until proven otherwise.

### What usually triggers a delay

Delays are common across the industry, and they are not automatically bad news. A studio that pushes a date is usually buying time for polish, certification, or a less crowded launch window. Community reports often frame any delay as a crisis, but a two-week slip is very different from a six-month rebuild. Watch the wording of the official statement — a "polish pass" reads very differently from a "scope change."
